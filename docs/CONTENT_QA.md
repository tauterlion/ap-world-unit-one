# Content review — V1

## Source and boundaries

The sole historical content source is the user-supplied `reference/AMSCO AP World History.pdf` (2022 copyright edition). The introductory historical-thinking material on printed pages xxii–xxiii informed the reasoning approach. No web sources or additional historical facts were used to fill gaps.

The bank uses the narrative and evidence in Topics 1.1–1.6, with background developments included only where those sections discuss them. Some questions therefore examine precedents before 1200, such as the Zanj Rebellion and earlier regional kingdoms. Later units and Topic 1.7 were not used to generate content. The c. 1200–1450 topic framing does not mean every background example begins in 1200.

## Editorial review

All 150 prompts, answers, and explanations were compared with their cited source passages during authoring and review. References are stored per question rather than only at board level. Checks addressed:

- Answer support and the explanation's causal or comparative claim.
- Important concepts rather than difficult-to-recall numerical trivia.
- Recall at $100–$200, relationships at $300, and interpretation/argument at $400–$500.
- Distinct questions: concepts recur where a higher-value question asks for a different reasoning operation, not merely the same factual answer.
- Category names that do not give away the expected recall answer.
- Short paraphrases, explicitly labeled; no invented primary-source quotation.
- Source limitations: al-Beruni's generalizations, male-authored records about women, and Spanish accounts of Aztec sacrifice are treated as evidence to assess.
- Qualified answers where the text qualifies a claim, including potential bias, limited mobility, and mixed causes.

The textbook's encoded PDF text contains damaged characters in some names and dates. Names were normalized using the surrounding narrative, and questions avoid dependence on corrupted numerical details. Visual inspection of a rendered source page confirmed its printed pagination and narrative structure.

Some source sections include broad or debatable formulations. The bank avoids expanding them with outside knowledge, and generally selects supported institutional relationships over disputed details. Answers are model responses for self-grading; they are not exhaustive rubrics or official College Board exam questions.

## Automated validation

Tests verify six boards, five categories per board, five ordered values per category, 150 unique IDs and prompts, required answer/explanation fields, source-page boundaries, printed-to-PDF offsets, and valid table structure. These mechanical checks supplement editorial source review; they do not independently prove historical accuracy.

See each question's `source` object for the question-to-source mapping. In-game answer panels show the printed page citation. The textbook file is excluded from public builds.
